# Field Jog Go

Handheld. One round screen, one fader, four buttons. It is the unit you take on the train.

The shell is 150 × 74 × 16 mm. An Elegoo Mars 5 Ultra plate is 153.36 × 77.76 × 165 mm, so each half prints flat, face on the plate, with about 1.5 mm spare on the long side and 2 mm on the short side. Do not try to put both halves on one plate. The caps, the knob, the fader cap and the cream bezel share the second plate.

## Face

Engineering grey shell. Cream bezel around the glass. Black caps. Orange knob, orange fader cap, orange rule under the wordmark.

The GC9A01 sits in the upper middle. The hole is 33 mm, so the 32.4 mm glass shows and the 38 mm board stays under the skin. The pin tab points down, toward the fader. A 0.55 mm cream ring drops into the recess and finishes flush.

The encoder is the top-right bushing. It is an EC11 or anything with a 6 mm shaft. The knob is a friction fit with a flat for a D-shaft, and the flutes are 0.35 mm, which is what this printer is for.

The fader is the same pot as the 88 mm deck. 75 × 9 mm body, 60 mm of travel, dual-gang, B103. The slot is 62 × 3.2 mm. Two M2 screws pull it up to the lid. They do not come through the face. Rails in the tray stop it sliding sideways. Bend the pins into the channel in the floor.

Cue and Play are the two larger caps on the left, stacked, flush in a 1.7 mm recess. Under each is a 12 × 12 mm switch. Loop and Rev are the two smaller caps on the right of the fader. A 3 mm LED sits directly above each one, in a 3.15 mm hole with a shallow countersink so the lens finishes almost flush.

USB-C is a panel-mount cut on the left end, 11 × 4.6 mm, centered a little above the middle so the plug clears a hand on the buttons. The headphone jack is a 6.3 mm hole on the right end.

Four M2 screws come up through the tray into bosses in the lid. The face has no screw heads. The lid skirt drops 3.2 mm into the tray with 0.2 mm of air a side, which is the clearance this resin wants.

## Print

Grey resin for both halves. Orange for the knob and the fader cap. Black for the four caps. Cream for the bezel.

Put the outside face on the plate. The first layers are the face, and they come off glossy and flat. The cavity points at the vat. Support only the bosses, the skirt and the switch pockets. Tilt the long axis about 15° if the suction cup warning comes up; the face is only 150 × 74, which this machine handles flat.

Wash the pockets before cure. The lettering is 0.4 mm deep. A wash-out leaves it, and a toothpick of orange resin in the rule is the second color. The ticks around the glass are 0.28 mm wide. Do not sand the face.

The knob prints on its top, so the bore is open toward the vat. The fader cap prints grip-up. The bezel prints flat. No supports on any of those if you give them a brim of three layers.

## What it does not have

No platter, no MIDI jacks, no line in or out, no battery bay. USB-C powers it and carries the audio class if the firmware does. The headphone jack is the only analog hole. There is room in the tray, beside the screen and under the empty right cheek, for a small DAC and the ESP32-S3. A battery would mean a deeper tray, and the plate still allows it, but this pass is the thin one.
